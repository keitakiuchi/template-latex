# Reviewer-Comment Integration

Use this reference when reviewer comments drive manuscript or supplement
changes. Keep response-letter accountability separate from reader-facing
manuscript content: address every distinct comment in the response letter, but
change the manuscript only when the underlying problem requires it.

## Contents

- Placement Guide

## Minimum Sufficient Incorporation

Treat a reviewer comment as evidence of a possible scientific, reporting, or
reader-facing problem rather than as an instruction to reproduce the review
exchange. Identify the underlying problem before selecting an edit.

Use **minimum sufficient incorporation**: make the smallest set of
reader-facing changes that fully resolves the underlying problem. Do not equate
this standard with the shortest possible edit, and do not use it to omit a
material method, result, interpretation, limitation, or reporting obligation.

Separate distinct information units within a comment. Several comments may
identify one shared problem, and one comment may identify several different
problems. Map information units, not comment numbers, to manuscript locations.

## Placement Guide

Use these placements as defaults, not hard limits. Override them when journal
instructions, reporting standards, reproducibility, measurement integrity, or
reader comprehension requires a different placement.

| Underlying problem | Default primary reader-facing residence | Keep in the response letter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Translation, wording, labeling, or transcription error | Correct every affected authoritative occurrence; describe the general verification procedure in Methods only when it is methodologically relevant; give full item wording in a supplement or item table when needed | What was corrected, where it was corrected, and review-specific discovery or correction history that readers do not need |
| Missing method or procedural detail | Methods; use the supplement for extensive procedural material | The direct answer, reason for the addition, and exact change location |
| Missing result | Results; add it to the Abstract only when it is a principal result needed to represent the study accurately | Why it was added and where it now appears |
| Missing construct definition | Introduction for the conceptual definition; Methods for study-specific operationalization | The response position and locations of any coordinated edits |
| Missing interpretive boundary or alternative interpretation | Discussion or Limitations according to whether it changes interpretation or states a study constraint | Detailed rebuttal, rejected alternatives, and reviewer-facing rationale not needed by readers |
| Item-, version-, or decision-specific traceability | Supplement, item table, protocol, or other authoritative record when readers need the traceability; otherwise no manuscript change | Detailed chronology and provenance needed to answer the comment but not to interpret the study |
| Reader misunderstanding when the manuscript is already sufficient | No manuscript change, or one localized clarification if it materially reduces ambiguity | Why the existing text is sufficient or why the local clarification resolves the concern |
| Positive or acknowledgment-only comment | No manuscript change | A concise acknowledgment |

If a placement decision requires new evidence, analysis, methodology, claim
strength, manuscript architecture, or author response position, stop and obtain
that substantive decision before drafting the incorporation.

## Primary Residence and Section-Essential Echoes

Assign each distinct information unit one **primary residence**. Add a
**section-essential echo** elsewhere only when the receiving section cannot
perform its own function without a shorter version of that information.

Use this default placement budget:

- Put a methodological or analytical decision in Methods.
- Put the corresponding observed result in Results.
- Put its interpretation in Discussion.
- Put a material study constraint in Limitations.
- Put detailed item wording, version history, or traceability in the supplement
  or another authoritative record when readers need it.
- Put only study-level principal methods, results, interpretations, and
  boundaries in the Abstract or final Conclusion.

Do not count section-specific statements of different functions as repetition
merely because they share a topic. Audit instead for repeated reviewer-driven
rationale, revision chronology, defensive explanation, or the same unevaluated
matter recited across multiple editorial sections. Aligning sections does not
require repeating why a reviewer-driven revision was made.

## Three Incorporation Tests

### Reader-Need Test

Imagine removing the review exchange. Retain the proposed manuscript text only
if readers still need it to understand, evaluate, reproduce, or interpret the
study, or to satisfy a material reporting obligation.

### Peer-Item Parity Test

Compare the depth and frequency of the proposed explanation with analogous
items, measures, analyses, decisions, or limitations. Require a scientific,
methodological, reporting, or reader-facing reason for any marked asymmetry; do
not require mechanical equality.

### Response-Letter Voice Test

Check whether the proposed manuscript text explains the review exchange,
defends the authors' revision process, asserts that a choice was not post hoc,
or narrates why the revision sequence matters. Treat these features as review
signals rather than forbidden phrases. Retain the content only when readers
need its scientific or reporting substance, and rewrite it in manuscript voice.

## Final Integration Pass

- Account for every distinct reviewer concern in a response-letter unit.
- Confirm that response-letter units have not been mirrored mechanically as
  manuscript paragraphs.
- Confirm one primary residence for each information unit and justify every
  section-essential echo.
- Preserve all material evidence, methods, results, interpretations,
  limitations, and required disclosures.
- Remove review chronology, defensive rationale, and item-specific exceptional
  detail that lacks a reader-facing reason.
- Confirm that analogous content receives comparable treatment unless a
  documented scientific or reporting reason justifies the difference.
